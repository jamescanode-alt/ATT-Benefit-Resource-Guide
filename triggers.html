Financial Trigger Finder

Enter a client's date of birth, hire date, planned (or actual) separation date, and employee group, and get the dated list of financial triggers to review together: catch-up contributions, the Rule of 55, 59½, Social Security windows, the IRMAA lookback, Medicare enrollment, QCDs, RMDs, and the AT&T-specific Mod 75 and retiree-medical milestones.

Section 01 · Interactive

Client inputs

Start with the employee group and employment status, then choose whether to enter exact dates or age / years of service, whichever the advisor has on hand. Either way the tool computes the same trigger dates (age- and service-based entries just read as "on or about").

Read this before relying on the output

This tool maps generic, dated planning triggers from statutory rules and the SPD-verified rules in the three plan guides. It assumes continuous service from hire to separation, uses exact dates where the plans count completed whole years and Term of Employment, and cannot see breaks in service, rehires, disability provisions, or a client's actual contract. Indexed dollar figures are 2026 tax-year values. Always confirm a client's actual status in NetBenefits, the controlling SPD, and ssa.gov / medicare.gov before advising.

Section 02 · Output

Trigger timeline

Every dated trigger for this client, in order. Faded items have passed (still worth confirming they were handled); the gold item is the next one coming. Items flagged in red need attention before the separation date.

Section 03 · Output

Separation checklist

What the separation date itself triggers: the items to walk through when the client knows (or is choosing) their last day on payroll.

The advisor's lens

The separation date is the most controllable variable on this page. A client a few months short of a Mod 75 milestone, the Rule-of-55 year, or their vesting cliff is often trading away subsidized pension income, penalty-free 401(k) access, or retiree-medical eligibility, frequently a six-figure decision. Map the date against the timeline above before the client gives notice.

Section 04 · Reference

Age-trigger reference

The full trigger map behind the timeline. Statutory ages don't change yearly; the dollar figures are 2026 values and are catalogued for annual update in ANNUAL-TAX-FIGURES.md.

Age / eventTriggerWhat to review
3 yrs serviceVestingPension and 401(k) Company Match vest at 3 years. Separating earlier forfeits both.
Mod 75Age + service ≥ one of: any/30 · 50/25 · 55/20 · 65/10Union & Legacy Non-Bargained: early-retirement subsidy on the legacy pension. All groups incl. Mobility: the Medical Program uses the same combinations to gate retiree-medical enrollment.
50Catch-up contributions+$8,000 (2026) on top of the $24,500 402(g) limit, from the calendar year they turn 50.
55Rule of 55 · pension threshold ageSeparate in/after the calendar year they turn 55 → penalty-free AT&T 401(k) withdrawals (forfeited by IRA rollover). Also the age where the Mod 75 early-commencement reduction ends (56 for Southeast bargained contracts).
59½Penalty ends everywhere10% early-withdrawal penalty ends for 401(k)s and IRAs regardless of employment status.
60–63Super catch-up$11,250 (2026) replaces the standard catch-up for calendar years the client turns 60 through 63; reverts at 64.
62Social Security earliest claimPermanently reduced (~30% below an FRA-67 benefit); earnings test applies if still working. ssa.gov
63IRMAA lookback beginsMedicare premiums at 65 are set by MAGI from two years prior, so the year they turn 63 is the first year Roth conversions and gains can raise their first Medicare premiums. 2026 tiers start above $109,000 single / $218,000 MFJ. medicare.gov
64¾Medicare Initial Enrollment Period7-month window opens 3 months before the 65th-birthday month. Missing it (without employer coverage) means lifetime Part B penalties.
65Medicare · IRMAA · coverage handoffPre-65 bridge coverage (retiree group plan or COBRA) hands off to Medicare + supplement. COBRA does not count as employer coverage for delaying Part B. Mobility: also the plan's Normal Retirement Age.
FRA 66–67Social Security Full Retirement AgeBy birth year: 1954 or earlier 66; 1955–59 66 + 2 mo/yr; 1960+ 67. Unreduced benefit; earnings test ends. ssa.gov
70Delayed credits max out~8%/yr delayed-retirement credits stop at 70. No benefit to waiting longer.
70½QCDs availableQualified Charitable Distributions direct from IRAs (annual limit indexed); excluded from MAGI and count toward RMDs once those begin. irs.gov
73 / 75RMDs beginBorn 1951–1959: age 73. Born 1960 or later: age 75 (SECURE 2.0). First RMD may be delayed to April 1 of the following year (doubling two into one tax year). Roth IRAs and (since 2024) Roth 401(k)s are exempt. irs.gov

Born-1959 note: SECURE 2.0's text created ambiguity for 1959 births (73 vs 75); IRS proposed regulations resolve it to 73. Confirm final regs before relying on it for a 1959-born client.
