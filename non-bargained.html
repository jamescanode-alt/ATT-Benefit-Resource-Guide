The AT&T Legacy Non-Bargained Retirement Benefits Guide

What a new Farther advisor needs to read, explain, and advise on the pension and 401(k) for AT&T's legacy non-bargained (management) employees under the Nonbargained Program, from the "greatest of three formulas" pension structure to the decisions that matter most for clients. For AT&T Mobility employees, use the Mobility Program guide in the sidebar's plan selector.

Section 01

The benefit landscape

A retiring AT&T management employee may draw income from up to three layers. Understanding how they stack helps you frame the whole picture in a first meeting.

🏛️

AT&T Pension

Employer-funded lifetime benefit, paid as a monthly annuity or (for many) a lump sum. The core of this guide.

💼

AT&T 401(k)

Employee + employer money at Fidelity. Investable, portable, inheritable. Grows with the market.

🇺🇸

Social Security

The federal layer. Not an AT&T benefit, but it shapes claiming timing and the income gap you're solving for.

The advisor's lens

The pension and 401(k) play complementary roles. The pension is a guaranteed-floor / longevity asset; the 401(k) is the flexible, growth-and-legacy asset. A common planning move is to use guaranteed pension income to cover essential expenses, freeing the 401(k) to be invested for growth and used for discretionary spending and inheritance.

Accuracy first

Figures and tables here are drawn from the Nonbargained Program Summary Plan Description (SPD) in your project library. Specific factors, bands, and dollar limits change by plan year and by the client's company history. Always verify a client's actual numbers in NetBenefits before giving advice. This guide builds intuition; it is not a substitute for the controlling plan documents.

Section 03

Eligibility & vesting

Two gates control whether a client has a benefit at all: are they an Eligible Employee, and are they vested?

Who is eligible (Nonbargained Program)

You are an Eligible Employee if you are a Management Employee of a Participating Company and were employed by one on Dec 31, 2006 and were not rehired after that date. In plain English: certain legacy SBC management hired or rehired before Jan 1, 2007. Hired or rehired after that date generally means the Management Cash Balance Program instead.

Common exclusions

Not eligible here: bargaining-unit employees; nonmanagement-nonunion employees; leased employees and independent contractors; nonresident aliens with no U.S.-earned income; Puerto Rico residents; someone on a temporary/acting management promotion of ≤1 year; anyone already accruing under another program (e.g., AT&T Legacy Management, Mobility, or Southeast Management). Special transfer/promotion rules can apply.

How vesting works

Vesting is when the benefit becomes the employee's to keep, even if they leave.

RequirementThreshold
Years of Vesting Service3 years (100% vested), or reaching Normal Retirement Age while still an employee

One Year of Vesting Service is a 12-month Period of Service. Participation begins after one Year of Participation Service. Normal Retirement Age is age 65, or if later the 5th anniversary of the date plan participation began. (Different rules can apply if termination was before Jan 1, 2010.)

The advisor's lens

The 3-year vesting cliff is easy to clear for career employees, but a client considering leaving in their first few years should know whether they've crossed it. Far more consequential for this population is Modified Rule of 75 timing (Section 08), which governs early-retirement subsidies and retiree healthcare access.

Don't confuse this with the union structure

The union (bargained) guide describes a pension that adds a Cash Balance portion to a Pre-99 Pension Band portion. The Nonbargained pension is different: the three formulas compete, and the client receives only the single highest result, not a combination. Reading a management statement as if the pieces stack will overstate the benefit.

An updated career-average formula: 1.6% of CAM Income ÷ 12. For most management clients this is the controlling (highest) formula.

Cash Balance — frozen

PBM — narrow

The advisor's lens

You rarely need to compute all three by hand, NetBenefits reports the controlling benefit. What you do need to know is which formula is winning and why, because that determines whether the Modified Rule of 75 subsidies apply (CAM/PBM only) and whether a lump sum is available (needs a Cash Balance component or the post-2018 full-lump-sum rule).

Worked example

Suppose the pre-2000 piece is $900,000 (a $60,000 average annual compensation × 15 years of service as of 12/31/1999) and post-1999 Pension Compensation totals $1,200,000 over the rest of the career. CAM Income = $2,100,000.
Monthly benefit = 1.6% × $2,100,000 ÷ 12 = $2,800/month at Normal Retirement Age.

The advisor's lens

Because the post-1999 piece keeps adding actual Pension Compensation, CAM keeps growing every year the client works, and higher-comp years (bonuses, commissions, promotions) add directly to the base. This is the opposite of the frozen cash-balance formula, and it is why continuing to work is usually accretive for a Nonbargained client's pension, on top of any Modified Rule of 75 milestone.

Section 06

Cash balance formula (frozen)

Some Nonbargained clients, certain legacy SBC, PTG, and SNET participants, plus Ameritech managers hired, rehired, or promoted on/after Jan 1, 2001, also carry a Cash Balance Formula. For this population it behaves very differently from the union cash-balance benefit.

The key fact: pay credits are frozen

Before it was frozen, the account earned basic benefit credits (based on Pension Compensation) plus interest credits. On Jan 14, 2005, basic benefit credits stopped. Since then the account grows by interest credits only (the monthly equivalent of the 30-Year U.S. Treasury rate, from a specified prior month). No new pay-based credits accrue.

What we can and can't show

The SPD does not publish the historical age-credit-factor schedule for this frozen formula, it directs participants to the Recordkeeper for those details. So unlike the union guide, there is no age-credit-factor table to reproduce here, and there shouldn't be: for Nonbargained clients this is a historical, interest-only balance, not an active accrual engine. Pull the actual figure from NetBenefits.

Why it still matters: the lump sum

Even when CAM is the higher (controlling) formula, an existing Cash Balance component is what historically unlocked a lump sum option through the CAM Excess Calculation (Section 10). Since 2018 a broader full-lump-sum rule also exists, but a cash-balance component remains a common path to lump-sum eligibility for pre-2018 terminations.

The advisor's lens

Don't oversell the frozen balance as a growth asset, it isn't. Its planning value is twofold: it can make a client lump-sum eligible, and it sets the partial lump-sum amount when CAM is much larger (Section 10). Frame CAM as the pension's engine and the cash balance as an optionality lever on how the benefit can be paid.

Section 07

The PBM formula

The Pension Band Minimum (PBM) formula is narrow. It applies only to a PBM Eligible Participant: someone who was promoted into the Nonbargained Program from a Banded Program (Midwest, Southwest, or West) on/after June 13, 2001, and who had an accrued benefit under that banded program at the time.

For an eligible participant, the PBM benefit is the greater of two amounts:

Option (a)

Pension Calculation Service × the pension-band amount for the band you were promoted from, plus any Supplemental Monthly Pension Benefit accrued at the date of promotion.

Option (b)

The monthly benefit earned under your prior program at promotion, plus 1.2% of the Pension Compensation you earn in this Program ÷ 12.

There is no double-counting of any period of service. Remember this whole formula only becomes the client's benefit if it is higher than CAM and Cash Balance.

The advisor's lens

PBM mostly surfaces for a client who spent years in a craft/bargained band and then moved into management. When you see a former bargained-then-promoted history, check whether PBM is the winning formula, and note that (like CAM) it carries the Modified Rule of 75 early-retirement treatment.

Section 08

The Modified Rule of 75

"Mod 75" determines whether a client can retire early on the CAM or PBM formula without the steep age-based reduction, and it can also open the door to retiree healthcare. It is where advisors most often catch costly client misconceptions.

A client satisfies Mod 75 when their age and Term of Employment hit one of these combinations, tested on or before Termination of Employment:

AgeTerm of Employment required
Any age30 years
5025 years
5520 years
65 or older10 years

Eligibility uses completed whole years and is locked at termination, it cannot be earned afterward. Important: Mod 75 governs the CAM and PBM formulas only. If the Cash Balance Formula is the client's benefit, Mod 75 does not apply.

How Mod 75 changes the CAM / PBM benefit

  • No reduction at all once at/after age 55
  • Before 55: reduced only 0.25%/month (3%/yr), half the normal rate

Mod 75 met, under 30 years

Subsidized
  • No reduction once at/after age 55
  • Before 55: reduced 0.5%/month (6%/yr)

Does NOT meet Mod 75

Penalized
  • Benefit multiplied by the age-based early retirement factor (Appendix B)
  • Much steeper, factors rise sharply only in the late 40s to 60s

The reduction is measured against age 55 for everyone in this program (there is no 55/56 contract split as in the union guide). Unreduced commencement is at 55 if Mod 75 is met; the deferred-vested early-retirement factors are unreduced only at age 65.

Four traps to flag

1. It's specific pairs, not simple addition. "My age plus my service is 75, so I qualify" is the classic mistake, the combinations are fixed thresholds (any/30, 50/25, 55/20, 65/10), not any sum reaching 75.

2. You can't "age into" it after you leave. Eligibility is tested at termination. A vested-but-not-Mod-75 client who quits early is locked out of the subsidy permanently.

3. Meeting Mod 75 is not always "no reduction." Commencing before age 55 still costs 0.5%/mo, or 0.25%/mo with 30+ years. Unreduced applies at/after 55.

4. It doesn't touch a cash-balance-only benefit. If Cash Balance is the controlling formula, there is no Mod 75 subsidy to capture.

Retiree healthcare access: often the bigger prize

This is confirmed by the controlling document. The AT&T Medical Program SPD sets retiree ("Post-Employment") medical eligibility for a former Management, Bargained, or Nonmanagement-Nonunion employee on the same Modified Rule of 75 table, met at the time you terminate employment. In other words, the Mod 75 milestone that subsidizes the pension is also the gate to eligibility to buy into AT&T retiree medical. For many clients that access is worth more than the pension subsidy itself.

Why this matters most for pre-65 retirees

A client who retires before age 65 is not yet Medicare-eligible. Buying individual coverage in that gap can run well over $1,000–$2,000/month per person. The AT&T Medical Program is a pre-Medicare bridge: a Mod-75-eligible retiree can enroll until they become Medicare-eligible, at which point they transition off the active-style Program (to a private exchange, with possible Medicare Part B/Part D premium reimbursement). A client weighing an early exit who would lose that eligibility by leaving a few months short of Mod 75 is often making a six-figure mistake without realizing it.

For management, the subsidy is essentially gone

Meeting Mod 75 opens eligibility to enroll, it does not fix the price. The retiree pays a monthly contribution toward the Cost of Coverage, and for management the Company subsidy has been largely eliminated:

Hired on/after 1/1/2001 → 100% of cost (no subsidy), always. Same if rehired on/after 1/1/2020. Only pre-2001 hires may qualify, under legacy-company rules (Legacy S/T/C).

Pre-Medicare (under-65) subsidy: eliminated for management whose last day on payroll is on/after 1/1/2022 (i.e., you had to retire by 12/31/2021). Announced Dec 15, 2020.

65+ Medicare subsidy: eliminated for anyone (management or bargained) with a last day on/after 1/1/2021.

Net: a manager retiring today generally buys in at full cost (enrolled through Alight) with no HRA. Contributions, plan tiers (Bronze/Silver/Gold), and dental/life/LTC live in the separate Medical Program SPD and change over time, confirm the client's actual contribution in their Total Reward statement. A separate legacy Pension-Based Eligibility path also exists for certain long-tenured former management whose service pension was granted before old regional cutoff dates (1996–2008).

How the pension gets paid

At retirement the client chooses a form of payment. The choice is permanent (once payments begin it cannot be changed), so this is a decision to get right the first time.

How the annuity amounts relate

The Nonbargained Program offers all four annuity forms, using fixed percentages of the Single Life Annuity. Using the SPD's $800/month single-life example:

Defaults & consent

If the client has a spouse at commencement, the default is the 50% Joint & Survivor annuity. Electing anything else (single life, 100% J&S, or lump sum) requires notarized spousal consent. If unmarried, the default is single life. J&S may be waived pursuant to a QDRO in this program.

Lump sum: two paths to eligibility

Full lump sum (post-2018 rule)

If the client terminated on/after May 25, 2018 and has a Benefit Commencement Date on/after June 1, 2018, they may take the entire Pension Benefit as a lump sum, no monthly pension.

CAM Excess Calculation (older path)

For a CAM/PBM benefit that also has a Cash Balance component, the CAM Excess Calculation determines how much can be taken as a lump sum and how much, if any, stays a monthly annuity.

Worked example: CAM Excess Calculation

CAM benefit = $1,000/month, worth $180,000 as a lump sum. Cash Balance account = $90,000. Difference = $90,000, which converts back to a $500/month annuity.
Because that excess is ≥ $400/month, the client takes a partial lump sum of $90,000 (the cash-balance amount) plus a residual monthly annuity (SLA, 50% or 75% J&S) for the rest.
If the converted difference were < $400/month, the client could take the entire benefit as a lump sum.

Small-benefit cash-out

If the present value of the pension is ≤ $7,000 at termination, the normal election rules don't apply, the plan automatically distributes or rolls over the benefit (amounts ≤ $1,000 as a single lump sum; over $1,000 to $7,000 as an automatic IRA rollover unless the client elects otherwise). This threshold rose from $5,000 to $7,000 effective Jan 1, 2024 (2024 SMM). Flag this so a client isn't surprised.

The advisor's lens: annuity vs. lump sum

This is the marquee pension decision. The annuity is a longevity hedge with a guaranteed floor and no market risk; the lump sum offers control, growth potential, flexibility, and a legacy asset but transfers investment and longevity risk to the client. Weigh health and family longevity most heavily, then other guaranteed income, spousal needs, legacy goals, and the annuity's implied payout rate versus what a diversified portfolio could support. There is no universal right answer, but there's a right answer for this client.

Tax mechanic to know

A lump sum paid directly to the client triggers a mandatory 20% federal withholding. A direct rollover to an IRA or other eligible plan avoids withholding and preserves tax deferral, usually the cleaner path when a lump sum is chosen.

Timing the lump sum around interest rates

When a CAM or PBM benefit is taken as a lump sum, it is the annuity converted to a present value using the IRS minimum present-value segment rates, so it moves inversely to interest rates: rising rates shrink the lump sum, falling rates grow it. AT&T locks the prior-November rates for the whole commencement calendar year, so the year a client commences can materially change the payout, this bites hardest on a full-lump-sum election. The frozen cash-balance component, by contrast, is simply its account value and isn't rate-converted. Model the client's likely commencement-year rate rather than defaulting the calculator, see irs.gov: minimum present-value segment rates.

Section 11 · The Savings Plan

The AT&T 401(k)

The defined-contribution side is the AT&T Retirement Savings Plan (SPD NIN 78-63233), administered by Fidelity through NetBenefits. The employee funds it and directs the investments, and AT&T adds a company match. Management employees are Eligible Employees, and match eligibility begins upon hire (no service wait).

Company match for management

Match applies only to Basic Contributions up to 6% of Compensation, and the formula depends on management hire date:

Hired / rehired on or after Jan 1, 2015: 133⅓% of the first 3% plus 100% of the next 3%, a maximum match of about 7% of pay for a 6% deferral.

Otherwise (earlier management hires): 80% of the first 6%, a maximum match of about 4.8% of pay.

Catch-up contributions are not matched. The match is invested in AT&T Shares by default but can be diversified immediately. Match vests 100% after 3 Years of Service (and automatically at death, disability, or age 65). Always confirm the client's specific match tier and current-year figures in NetBenefits.

Catch-up contributions: now Roth for higher earners

Clients age 50+ can make additional elective deferrals above the standard limit, with an enhanced "super catch-up" for those ages 60–63. Under a SECURE 2.0 change, catch-up contributions must be made as Roth for participants whose prior-year FICA wages exceeded $150,000 (indexed).

Timing note for management plans

Unlike a collectively bargained plan (which gets a later compliance deadline), a non-bargained management plan is generally subject to the standard Roth-catch-up effective date. Confirm how and when the AT&T savings plan has adopted the rule, but don't assume the union's delayed timeline applies to this population.

The advisor's lens

For most clients the 415(c) limit is invisible, match plus modest deferrals never approach it. But for a high-earning, high-saving management client, the gap between their deferrals + match and the $72,000 total is a large, underused Roth opportunity. The sequence to coach: capture the full match every pay period → max the 402(g) deferral (pre-tax or Roth per career stage) → fill remaining 415(c) room with after-tax contributions → convert those to Roth promptly.

The four funding buckets

A 401(k) account is built from four distinct funding sources. Understanding how employee choices interact with employer money is essential for maximizing the plan.

①

Pre-tax

Deducted before tax; grows tax-deferred; taxed as ordinary income at withdrawal. Counts toward the elective-deferral limit.

②

Roth

Contributed after tax; qualified growth and withdrawals are tax-free. Also counts toward the elective-deferral limit.

③

After-tax (non-Roth)

A separate, often-overlooked bucket above the deferral limit. Contributed after tax; only the growth is taxable, which is exactly why it pairs with conversion.

④

Employer Match

Company-funded matching dollars up to a plan cap. Helps build baseline wealth automatically.

Pre-tax vs. Roth: a career-stage decision

Whether a client should direct 401(k) contributions to pre-tax or Roth turns largely on their tax rate now versus their expected tax rate when the money comes out. Career stage is usually the best proxy.

Early career / lower-earning years → lean Roth

A younger or lower-paid employee is often in a low marginal bracket, so the deduction from a pre-tax contribution is worth little. Paying tax now at a low rate and letting decades of growth come out tax-free is frequently the stronger trade. Roth dollars also don't add to MAGI in retirement, which helps with future IRMAA and Social Security taxation.

Peak-earning years → lean pre-tax

A senior manager at the top of the pay scale may be in a high bracket where the pre-tax deduction is genuinely valuable. Deferring now and drawing later (potentially in lower-income early-retirement years before pension/Social Security/RMDs stack up) can lower the lifetime tax bill.

It's rarely all-or-nothing. Tax diversification (holding both pre-tax and Roth buckets) gives the client levers to manage MAGI year by year in retirement (e.g., drawing from Roth in a year they'd otherwise cross an IRMAA tier).

After-tax contributions → the "mega backdoor Roth"

The after-tax bucket is powerful because of what you do next: the client can convert after-tax dollars to Roth using the plan's Roth In-Plan Rollover feature. Converting right away means little or no growth has accrued yet, so there's little or no tax on the conversion, and from that point the money grows tax-free. The longer after-tax dollars sit unconverted, the more taxable growth builds up, so the move is to convert promptly and regularly.

The AT&T plan supports this

The AT&T Retirement Savings Plan SPD confirms both prerequisites: after-tax contributions are permitted (Basic Contributions can be before-tax, Roth, after-tax, or any combination, up to 50% of Compensation), and a Roth In-Plan Rollover lets you convert eligible before-tax and after-tax money to Roth within the plan. Note the conversion is irrevocable. Confirm the client's current elections and any plan limits in NetBenefits before building a strategy around it.

Watch the per-pay-period match trap

Front-loading can forfeit match

The plan matches Basic Contributions up to 6% of Compensation and the SPD describes no "true-up" to retroactively restore missed match. So if a client uses a large bonus or aggressive early-year deferrals to hit the elective-deferral limit before year-end, their contributions stop, and so does the per-paycheck match for the rest of the year, leaving employer money on the table. The safe move is to spread deferrals across all pay periods so at least 6% is contributed every period. Confirm timing in NetBenefits before a client front-loads.

Also note: the employer match applies only to pre-tax and Roth deferrals, not to after-tax (bucket ③) contributions. After-tax dollars are for getting more money into the plan once the match and deferral limit are captured, not for earning additional match.

At separation: two traps to handle

The Rule of 55, and how a rollover forfeits it

If a client separates from AT&T in the year they turn 55 or older, they can take withdrawals from the AT&T 401(k) penalty-free (no 10% early-withdrawal penalty) while the money stays in the plan, a valuable bridge for an early retiree before age 59½. Rolling the 401(k) to an IRA forfeits this: an IRA has no Rule of 55, so the client is back to the 59½ threshold. Before recommending a full rollover for a 55–59 client, check whether they'll need penalty-free access first, it can be worth leaving some or all in the plan. Note the plan may also limit how those withdrawals can be taken (e.g., not flexible monthly payments), confirm with Fidelity.

Handle an outstanding 401(k) loan before rolling over

If the client has a 401(k) loan when they leave, address it deliberately, three paths: (1) Pay it off before the rollover (allow ~72 hours for the $0 balance to post, or the rollover can inadvertently trigger a default); (2) Let it default, the outstanding balance is issued on a 1099-R as a taxable distribution (plus the 10% penalty if under 59½ with no exemption); or (3) Keep it by leaving enough balance in the plan and continuing payments. Flag this early, an overlooked loan is a common, avoidable tax surprise at rollover.

The advisor's lens

Farther's program is built specifically for AT&T employees, we manage funds held at Fidelity and consult across the full benefits stack (pension, 401(k), medical). The 401(k) is where ongoing investment management lives and is the natural complement to the pension's guaranteed income. Make sure contribution rate, match capture, and allocation are all working before optimizing anything fancier.

Section 12

IRMAA planning

Coordinating pension & 401(k) income with IRMAA in mind

Once a client reaches Medicare, their premiums are no longer flat. The Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA) adds a surcharge to Medicare Part B and Part D premiums when modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) crosses a threshold. Pension income, 401(k) withdrawals, and Roth conversions all feed MAGI, so how a client sequences distributions directly drives what they pay for Medicare.

Three features make IRMAA a planning problem rather than an afterthought:

FeatureWhat it means for planning
Two-year lookback2026 premiums are set by 2024 MAGI. A distribution or conversion today shows up in premiums ~2 years later, so plan the income, then watch the downstream year.
Cliff, not a rampCrossing a threshold by even $1 triggers the full surcharge for that tier, there's no phase-in. The dollar that puts a client over can carry a very high effective marginal cost.
Per personFor a married couple, both Medicare-enrolled spouses pay the surcharge once joint MAGI crosses a tier.

For reference, 2026 IRMAA begins above $109,000 MAGI (single) and $218,000 (married filing jointly), on a five-tier sliding scale (topping out at $500,000 single / $750,000 MFJ). Because the thresholds and surcharge amounts change yearly, always pull the current figures rather than relying on a remembered number: medicare.gov: Medicare costs & IRMAA.

The advisor's lens

A retiree in, say, the 22% federal bracket who nudges over an IRMAA line can face a combined effective marginal rate well above the headline bracket once the surcharge is counted. The practical move is to project MAGI for each year and size distributions and conversions to stay just under the next tier, especially in the years before age 65 and in the lookback years feeding the first Medicare premiums. Note: a voluntary Roth conversion or asset sale is not a qualifying event for an IRMAA appeal, so it has to be planned, not appealed.

Roth conversions with brackets and IRMAA in view

Converting pre-tax dollars to Roth means voluntarily recognizing income now to buy tax-free growth and future MAGI control. The window that often makes this attractive for AT&T clients is the gap between retiring and the onset of Social Security and required minimum distributions, a stretch of unusually low taxable income, especially for someone who retired early under Mod 75.

How to size a conversion

The disciplined approach is to "fill the bracket": convert just enough to use up a low or moderate tax bracket without spilling into the next one, and, once the client is within two years of Medicare or already enrolled, without crossing the next IRMAA tier. Because both the bracket and the IRMAA threshold are cliffs of a sort, the last dollars converted are the ones to watch. Model the conversion's MAGI impact two years forward for the IRMAA effect, not just the current-year tax.

Not advice: a framework

Conversion and contribution strategy depend on the individual's full tax picture, state taxes, legacy goals, and assumptions about future rates. Use these as factors to evaluate with the client and their tax professional, not as a default recommendation. Farther advisors are not providing tax advice.

The Retiree Death Benefit

A feature that exists in the Nonbargained Program but not in the Mobility or cash-balance programs, and one clients rarely know they have. It is a separate lump-sum death benefit, over and above any pension survivor annuity.

Eligibility is narrow and date-driven

Only certain long-tenured retirees qualify, and the tests are company-specific (Ameritech, Pacific Telesis Group, SNET, Southwestern Bell each have their own dates). Typical patterns: began a service or disability pension on/before a mid-1990s date and were not rehired after a stated date; or satisfied Mod 75 (or began a disability pension) before a stated date and terminated by a stated year. This is a "check the client's specific company and dates" benefit, not a general one.

How much it pays

The amount is based on Wages (base pay for full-time service as of a company-specific date, or termination if earlier). The maximum is the greater of:

Option (a)

One times Wages, reduced 10% for each full year since Termination of Employment. (Some employees aren't subject to the reduction, confirm with the Recordkeeper.)

Option (b)

The monthly Pension Benefit × 12.

Who receives it

Paid as a lump sum to Eligible Beneficiaries in a fixed order (the regular beneficiary-designation rules do not govern it):

Spouse

If living with the retiree at the time of death.

Legally Recognized Partner

If living with the retiree at death.

Unmarried dependent children under 23

Or over 23 and incapable of self-support (restrictions apply).

Dependent parents

Living with the retiree or in a home the retiree provides.

Two things to flag for clients

Claims deadline: all claims must be made within one year of death, an easy benefit for a grieving family to miss. Make sure the household knows it exists. The 10% annual decay on Option (a) means its value erodes the longer it's been since termination, which can flip which option controls over time.

Section 14 · Advisor Toolkit

Severance & surplus packages

AT&T regularly declares "surplus" and offers severance. For a management client, the severance decision is tightly bound to the Mod 75 timing analysis, sometimes the right move is to find another internal role rather than take the package.

Management (non-bargained) severance is governed by the AT&T Severance Pay Plan (a separate plan from the pension). It pays a lump-sum Severance Allowance on an involuntary (surplus/layoff) termination, in exchange for a signed General Release and Waiver.

The severance schedule

The allowance is a percentage of Annual Basic Pay based on Term of Employment: 4% at 0–1 year, rising 4% per year, capped at 50% at 13+ years (e.g., 40% at 10 years). Annual Basic Pay is base pay (plus target incentive for sales roles); it excludes most group incentive pay. Paid within ~45 days of the signed release, and reduced by any legally required severance.

Term of EmploymentSeverance Allowance (% of Annual Basic Pay)
0–1 year4%
5 years20%
10 years40%
13 or more years50% (cap)

TOE is rounded to whole years (traditional rounding), except the 4% minimum is tested without rounding. Prior-severance and rehire offsets can reduce the recognized TOE, verify in the plan. Union/bargained severance is set by the collective bargaining agreement, not this plan.

The surplus-notice decision

When a client gets a surplus notice, they typically have a window to find another position in the company or take the package. For a client not yet Mod 75, staying (via a new internal role) to reach the milestone can be worth far more than the severance, it can unlock an unreduced service pension and retiree-medical eligibility. Map exact age and service against Mod 75 (Section 08) before advising on the package.

Tax & sequencing note

Severance is taxable as ordinary income in the year received (typically paid within ~45 days of separation) and cannot be rolled over, unlike a pension lump sum, which can be directly rolled to defer tax. Coordinate the severance, the pension election, and any 401(k) rollover in the same plan so the household's taxable income for the year is managed deliberately.

The advisor's lens

Severance is where the "should I leave now?" conversation gets real. It bundles a taxable cash payment with the Mod 75 pension/healthcare stakes and a hard decision window. Being the advisor who can model all three, package value, pension impact, and healthcare eligibility, is exactly the value-add that wins AT&T households.

Section 15

Strategy playbook

The recurring, high-value decisions where an AT&T management client benefits most from advice.

Confirm the program first

Nonbargained vs. Mobility vs. Management Cash Balance. This determines whether CAM/PBM and the Modified Rule of 75 even apply, or whether the benefit is a cash-balance account only.

Identify the winning formula

Pull NetBenefits and determine whether CAM, Cash Balance, or PBM is controlling. It drives early-retirement treatment and lump-sum availability.

Map age & service against Mod 75

Identify the nearest milestone (any/30, 50/25, 55/20, 65/10) and what reaching it is worth in reduced-penalty income and, often bigger, retiree-healthcare eligibility.

Run the annuity-vs-lump-sum analysis

Check lump-sum eligibility (post-2018 full lump sum, or CAM Excess Calculation). Compare guaranteed annuity income against a prudent withdrawal from the rolled-over lump sum, factoring health, longevity, spouse, and legacy.

Choose the survivor structure deliberately

SLA vs. 50/75/100% J&S is permanent and needs spousal consent. Remember the pop-up applies only if Mod 75 was met. Align with life insurance and the survivor's own income.

Don't forget the Retiree Death Benefit

For qualifying long-tenured legacy retirees, confirm eligibility and make sure the family knows about the one-year claims deadline.

Sequence the income layers

Coordinate pension start date, Social Security claiming age, and 401(k) withdrawals to manage taxes and bridge any early-retirement gap.

Maximize the 401(k), capture match, then go after-tax → Roth

Spread deferrals across every pay period, max the 402(g) deferral, then (if the plan allows) fill remaining 415(c) room with after-tax contributions and convert promptly. Match the career-stage pre-tax/Roth choice to the client's bracket.

Manage MAGI around IRMAA & brackets

In the pre-Medicare and lookback years, size withdrawals and Roth conversions to "fill the bracket" without crossing the next tax bracket or IRMAA tier, projecting MAGI two years forward.

First-meeting question set

Which legacy company, and when were you hired? · Are you management the whole time, or promoted from a bargained role? · Current age and Term of Employment? · Married, and what's your spouse's own income/coverage? · Target retirement age? · Health and longevity outlook? · What other savings and Social Security expectations do you have? These answers drive every recommendation above.

Section 16

Protections & rules to know

PBGC insurance

The pension is federally insured by the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation, covering normal/early retirement and certain survivor and pre-termination disability benefits, up to statutory limits. Lump sums forfeit this guarantee.

QDRO (divorce)

A Qualified Domestic Relations Order can assign part of the pension to a former spouse (and, in the Nonbargained Program, waive the J&S). Benefits otherwise can't be assigned, pledged, or garnished by creditors.

Rehire within AT&T

After a prior lump sum or annuity, rehire into a participating program reduces the post-rehire benefit by what was already received. A monthly annuity is not suspended on rehire into a management position, but is suspended on rehire into other positions.

Survivor benefits before commencement

For a vested CAM/PBM benefit, a spouse/partner may elect an SLA equal to 50% of the would-be benefit (a special unreduced version applies for active employees with 15+ years or who met Mod 75). Cash-balance survivors can take an annuity or a lump sum of the account.

Break-in-service rules

Before vesting, a break of 5+ years can wipe out prior vesting service; bridging rules cover shorter breaks. Once vested, breaks don't undo it. Layoff rehire within 2 years is generally not a break.

No double crediting

Service or pay can't count twice across plan programs; offsets prevent double counting for clients who moved between AT&T entities or programs. The MPA can port former Bell System service.

Section 17

Glossary

CAM Formula (Updated Career Average Minimum)
The primary Nonbargained formula: 1.6% of CAM Income ÷ 12. Still accrues with post-1999 Pension Compensation.
CAM Income
(Average annual Pension Compensation for the 5 years ending 12/31/1999 × service to 12/31/1999) + all Pension Compensation after 12/31/1999.
Cash Balance Formula (Nonbargained)
A hypothetical account whose basic benefit (pay) credits froze Jan 14, 2005; only interest credits (30-Year Treasury rate) continue. Can unlock a lump sum.
PBM Formula (Pension Band Minimum)
A minimum for participants promoted into management from a Banded Program (Midwest/Southwest/West) on/after Jun 13, 2001.
Greatest of
The Nonbargained pension equals the single highest of the CAM, Cash Balance, and PBM results, not their sum.
Pension Compensation
Monthly wages counted for the pension: base pay, group and individual incentive pay, certain special/lump-sum and officer-incentive payments, and STD paid while employed.
Modified Rule of 75 (Mod 75)
Age/service combinations (any/30, 50/25, 55/20, 65/10) that reduce or remove the early-commencement reduction on CAM/PBM and can open retiree-healthcare eligibility. Tested at termination.
Early Retirement Factor
The age-based fraction of the age-65 benefit applied when Mod 75 is not met (Appendix B of the SPD).
Normal Retirement Age
Age 65, or if later the 5th anniversary of plan participation.
Term of Employment
The service measure used for Mod 75 and vesting.
Pension Calculation Service
Continuous service with a Participating Company used in the CAM/PBM formulas (excludes service credited under another plan).
CAM Excess Calculation
The method that splits a CAM/PBM-plus-Cash-Balance benefit into a lump-sum portion and a residual annuity.
Full Lump Sum
Option to take the entire pension as a lump sum if terminated on/after May 25, 2018 with a BCD on/after June 1, 2018.
Joint & Survivor (J&S)
An annuity continuing 50/75/100% to a survivor. Nonbargained percentages of SLA: 90/85/80%. Pop-up applies only if Mod 75 was met.
Pop-up
Restoration of the retiree's benefit to the SLA level if the survivor dies first, available on Nonbargained J&S only when Mod 75 was met.
Retiree Death Benefit
A Nonbargained-only lump-sum death benefit for qualifying long-tenured legacy retirees, based on Wages; claims within one year of death.
PBGC
Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation, federal insurer of the pension.
QDRO
Qualified Domestic Relations Order, court order splitting the benefit in divorce.
SPD
Summary Plan Description, the controlling plain-language plan document.
402(g) limit
The annual cap on an employee's own elective deferrals (pre-tax + Roth). $24,500 in 2026.
415(c) limit
The annual cap on all additions to the plan combined, deferrals, employer match, and after-tax contributions. $72,000 in 2026.
Mega-backdoor Roth
Making after-tax contributions and converting them to Roth (in-plan or to a Roth IRA), turning 415(c) headroom into tax-free Roth money.
IRMAA
Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount, an income-based surcharge on Medicare Part B and D premiums, set by MAGI from two years prior.
MAGI
Modified adjusted gross income, the income measure that drives IRMAA tiers; fed by pension income, 401(k) withdrawals, and Roth conversions.
Roth conversion
Moving pre-tax dollars to Roth by recognizing income now in exchange for tax-free growth and lower future MAGI.
