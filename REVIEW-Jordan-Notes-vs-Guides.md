## ✅ A4 (retiree-medical subsidy) — now CONFIRMED by controlling documents

The **Medical Program SPD (2021)** plus the official **"AT&T Benefit changes 12-2020"** announcement
give a controlling-document basis. The precise, citable rules:

**Coverage (the right to enroll)** — unchanged: gated by the **Modified Rule of 75** at termination
(same table), for Management, Bargained, and NMNU former employees.

**Subsidy (whether the Company pays part of the premium)** — separate and now very restrictive:
- **Original Hire Date on/after 1/1/2001 → pay 100% of Cost of Coverage** (no subsidy), always.
  Same if **rehired on/after 1/1/2020**. *(Medical SPD, "Management/Bargained Contribution Rules,"
  General Rules 1–2.)*
- **Hired before 1/1/2001 (not rehired ≥1/1/2020)** → may get a subsidy under **Legacy Company**
  rules (Legacy S: hire before 8/1/97; Legacy T: 5+ yrs TOE by 12/31/1999; Legacy C: Cingular
  transition group; Legacy B: hire before 1/1/2001).
- **Two eliminations** (AT&T Benefit changes 12-2020):
  - **Pre-Medicare subsidy** (under 65): eliminated for **Management** employees whose **last day on
    payroll is on/after 1/1/2022** (announced 12/15/2020). → This is the "retire by end of 2021"
    cutoff in Jordan's notes, **confirmed**.
  - **Medicare (65+) subsidy**: eliminated for **both bargained and non-bargained** employees whose
    **last day is on/after 1/1/2021** (announced Dec 2019).

**Correction to Jordan's notes:** "Union will still get the subsidy" is only *half* right. Union keeps
**pre-Medicare** subsidy eligibility under the legacy rules (the 1/1/22 cutoff was management-only),
**but union lost the post-65 Medicare subsidy** for a last day on/after 1/1/2021, same as management.

**Recommended guide language (verified, ready to implement):**
- Retiree medical = *eligibility to enroll* via Mod 75; **subsidy is separate and mostly gone**.
- Anyone **hired on/after 1/1/2001 pays 100% of cost** (no subsidy) — this is most of the current
  workforce.
- **Management** who didn't retire by **12/31/2021** get **no pre-Medicare subsidy**.
- The **65+ Medicare subsidy is gone** for anyone (union or management) with a last day on/after
  **1/1/2021**.
- Enrollment/administration runs through **Alight** (Jordan's "A-Light Retiree Health Solution").
- Keep the hedge that exact contributions/plan tiers (Bronze/Silver/Gold) change yearly and are in
  the Medical SPD; the SPD shows illustrative 2021 monthly contributions (e.g., unsubsidized
  retiree-only Bronze ≈ $728–$1,136/mo depending on administrator).

## 🆕 NEW-1. Mandatory cash-out threshold raised to $7,000 (pension) — factual correction

The **July 2024 SMM (NIN 78-72720)** amends **every** pension program SPD (Union/bargained,
Nonbargained, Mobility, etc.), effective **Jan 1, 2024**:
- PV **≤ $1,000** → automatic single lump sum (or rollover by election).
- PV **> $1,000 and ≤ $7,000** → automatic direct rollover to an IRA unless the participant elects
  otherwise. *(Was $5,000.)*

**Guides today are out of date:**
- `non-bargained.html` (small-benefit cash-out): says **≤ $5,000** / "over $1,000 to $5,000."
- `mobility.html` (small-benefit cash-out): says "over $1,000 to $5,000."
- `index.html`: vague ("e.g., $1,000, $5,000, or $7,000").

**Recommendation:** Update all three to the current **$7,000** rule. Clean, controlling-doc-backed
correction — low risk, high accuracy value.

## 🆕 NEW-2. Severance Pay Plan (management / non-bargained) — potential new content

The **AT&T Severance Pay Plan (eff. 4/15/25)** is an **involuntary severance plan for non-bargained
(management) employees**. Core terms:
- **Severance Allowance = a % of Annual Basic Pay by Term of Employment**: 4% at 0–1 yr, +4%/yr,
  **capped at 50% at 13+ years** (40% at 10 yrs). *(≈ Jordan's "2 weeks/yr, ~6-month max.")*
- Requires a signed **General Release and Waiver**; paid within ~45 days; reduced by any legally
  required severance; special rehire/repayment offsets.
- Triggered by **involuntary** (surplus/layoff) termination.

**Why it matters:** Severance interacts directly with the **Mod 75 "should I leave now?"** decision
the guides already discuss (surplus notices, choosing severance vs. finding a new internal role to
hit a Mod 75 milestone). It is a **separate plan** and currently in **no** guide.

**Recommendation:** Consider a short **"If you're offered a severance / surplus package"** section on
the **Legacy Non-Bargained** guide (management-only; union severance is set by the CBA, not this
plan), tying it to the Mod 75 timing analysis. Scope decision is yours — flagging, not implementing.
The `Severance/` folder also holds "Resources for Employees Leaving AT&T" and internal layoff notes
that could seed it.

## Priority A — Substantive gaps worth adding (high client value)

### A1. Interest-rate / IRS segment-rate timing of the lump sum  *(Union + Legacy Non-Bargained)*
**Notes say (lines 9–49, 91–96, 135, 540):** A defined-benefit lump sum is the annuity converted
using the IRS **minimum present-value segment rates**. **Rates up → lump sum down** (inverse). AT&T
locks the **prior-November** rate for the entire following calendar year, so the year you commence
can swing the lump sum materially (their example: a jump from 2.72% to 5.83% ≈ ~30% smaller lump
sum). This is a major "*when* to retire / commence" lever.
**Guides today:** The annuity-vs-lump-sum sections cover withholding, PBGC, and payout-rate logic
but say **nothing** about interest-rate timing.
**Recommendation:** Add a short callout ("Timing the lump sum around interest rates") to the payment
sections of the **Union** and **Legacy Non-Bargained** guides. Explicitly note it does **not** apply
to Mobility/cash-balance (see A-note in B2). Link the IRS segment-rate page already cited in the
notes: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/minimum-present-value-segment-rates
**Verify:** the "prior-November, locked for the calendar year" mechanic against the current SPD/plan
admin; the 2.72/5.83 numbers are 2021–22 illustrations and should be presented as generic, not
current.

### A2. The "Rule of 55" / post-55 exemption on the 401(k)  *(all three guides)*
**Notes say (lines 112, 196–198, 712–714):** If you separate in the **year you turn 55 or older**,
you can take 401(k) withdrawals **penalty-free** while the money stays in the plan — **but rolling
the 401(k) to an IRA forfeits this** (an IRA has no Rule of 55; you're back to 59½). Some
recordkeepers also limit *how* you can take those withdrawals.
**Guides today:** The 401(k) "at separation" guidance covers rollovers and the 20% withholding but
**not** the Rule of 55 or the trade-off it creates with an IRA rollover.
**Recommendation:** Add this to the 401(k) separation/rollover discussion on all three pages — it's a
genuine, common early-retiree decision and it interacts directly with the rollover recommendation.
**Verify:** current IRS rule wording and note the AT&T plan's actual distribution flexibility.

### A3. Handling an outstanding 401(k) loan at separation/rollover  *(all three guides)*
**Notes say (lines 107–114):** Options are (a) **pay it off** before rolling over (allow ~72 hours
for the $0 balance to post, or you can inadvertently default), (b) **default** → the balance is
issued on a **1099-R** as a distribution (penalty if under 59½ and no exemption), or (c) **keep** the
loan by leaving enough balance in the plan.
**Guides today:** Not covered.
**Recommendation:** Add a brief "if the client has a 401(k) loan" note to the separation/rollover
guidance. This is a concrete trap that can trigger unexpected tax.
**Verify:** the specific mechanics against the AT&T Retirement Savings Plan / Fidelity process (the
"1.25× / keep enough balance" figure in the notes is anecdotal).

### A4. Retiree-medical subsidy has changed — managers vs. union  *(highest-impact accuracy item)*
**Notes say (lines 366–398):** The **pre-Medicare subsidy** (historically ~**$800/month** off
premiums before 65) was **eliminated for managers** unless they **retired by the end of 2021** — a
company-wide change, **regardless of Mod 75**. **Union employees still get the subsidy** currently.
The **HRA is part of the subsidy**, so no subsidy → no HRA. Structure described as "subsidy for the
~10 pre-65 years, then HRA for the ~10 post-65 years." Enrollment runs through **Alight** ("A-Light
Retiree Health Solution").
**Guides today:**
- **Legacy Non-Bargained** already says "eligible to *enroll*, subsidy varies by group/hire date,
  up to 100% of cost." That is directionally correct and consistent with the notes — but the notes
  add the concrete **end-of-2021 manager cutoff**, which would sharpen it considerably.
- **Union** guide's retiree-healthcare framing is more optimistic ("subsidized retiree medical,"
  "bridge at a fraction of open-market cost"). Per the notes, that's still **accurate for union**,
  but the guide doesn't state the **manager-vs-union divergence**.
**Recommendation:** This is the most valuable and most sensitive item. Consider (a) sharpening the
Legacy Non-Bargained retiree-medical callout with the 2021 manager cutoff, (b) noting on the Union
guide that the subsidy is a union-side benefit that management largely lost, and (c) adding **Alight**
to the retiree-medical/contacts content.
**Verify FIRST (compliance-sensitive):** confirm the end-of-2021 manager cutoff, the "$800,"
and the 10-yr subsidy → 10-yr HRA structure against the **current Medical Program SPD and the
benefits center** before publishing. The notes are informal and this is exactly the kind of
time-sensitive, benefit-defining claim we should not restate verbatim.
