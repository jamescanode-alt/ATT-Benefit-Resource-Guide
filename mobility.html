The AT&T Mobility Program Retirement Benefits Guide

What a new advisor needs to read, explain, and advise on the pension and 401(k) for AT&T Mobility employees. The pension here is simpler than the legacy plans: a single cash balance account, no "Rule of 75," no competing formulas.

Section 01

The benefit landscape

A retiring AT&T Mobility employee may draw income from up to three layers. Understanding how they stack helps you frame the whole picture in a first meeting.

🏛️

AT&T Pension

An employer-funded cash balance account, payable as a lump sum or an annuity. Simpler than the legacy AT&T pensions. The core of this guide.

💼

AT&T 401(k)

Employee + employer money at Fidelity. Investable, portable, inheritable. Grows with the market.

🇺🇸

Social Security

The federal layer. Not an AT&T benefit, but it shapes claiming timing and the income gap you're solving for.

The advisor's lens

Because the Mobility pension is a cash balance account (an easily-understood, lump-sum-eligible value), the planning center of gravity shifts toward the 401(k) and the rollover decision more than in the legacy plans. There is no early-retirement subsidy to time and no "greatest of" formula to decode. Your value is in the investment, tax, and sequencing work.

Accuracy first

Figures here are drawn from the Mobility Program Summary Plan Description (SPD) in your project library. Interest crediting rates, annuity factors, and dollar limits change by plan year. Always verify a client's actual account value and figures in NetBenefits before giving advice. This guide builds intuition; it is not a substitute for the controlling plan documents.

Client example

Meet the client we'll follow through this guide. Tanya Brooks is a CWA Orange Contract customer-service rep at AT&T Mobility, hired in 2010. Her entire pension is a single Cash Balance Account, no Pre-99 band, no "greatest of" formula, and no Modified Rule of 75 on the pension itself. That shifts her planning center of gravity to the 401(k), the rollover of that account, and the healthcare milestone, which is exactly where we'll spend our time.

Section 02

Who's covered & vesting

The Mobility Program is a program of the AT&T/WarnerMedia Pension Benefit Plan covering AT&T Mobility employees, a mix of bargained and legacy management.

Who is an Eligible Employee

You are generally in the Mobility Program if you are an employee of a Participating Company (AT&T Mobility Services LLC) and are either:

Bargained

A Bargaining Unit Employee covered by the applicable collective bargaining agreement, the CWA Orange Contract (Districts 1, 2-13, 4, 7, and 9).

Legacy management

A Management Employee employed by a Participating Company on Dec 31, 2005 and not rehired on/after Jan 1, 2006.

Common exclusions & boundaries

Management hired/rehired after Dec 31, 2005 generally falls under the Management Cash Balance Program, not here. Also excluded: leased employees, independent contractors, nonresident aliens with no U.S. income, Puerto Rico residents, and anyone already eligible under the Legacy Management, Nonbargained, or Southeast Management programs. Various bargained transfer-ins have their own dated rules.

If the client has moved between union and management

The Mobility Program covers both bargained and legacy management employees, so a Mobility client can cross that line without leaving this program at all. When they do move to a different program, the rule is the one common to every program of the Plan: "your Cash Balance Account will remain in this Program and will continue to earn Interest Credits," and at termination the client draws separate benefits from each program they accrued under. The account keeps compounding but stops receiving the 5% pay credit.

Because a transfer inside the AT&T Controlled Group is not a Termination of Employment, Term of Employment (seniority) runs straight through, which preserves progress toward retiree-healthcare eligibility. On the savings side, the 401(k) account does not follow the client either: it freezes in place and a new account opens in the other plan. The full treatment, including the three-year service bridge into the Nonbargained Program and the cash-balance carve-out that cancels it, is in the management guide, Section 11 and the union guide, Section 09.

Vesting

RequirementThreshold
Years of Vesting Service3 years (100% vested), or reaching Normal Retirement Age while still an employee

Participation begins after one Year of Participation Service. Normal Retirement Age is a flat age 65, there is no 5th-anniversary extension as in the legacy Nonbargained Program.

Client example: Tanya Brooks

Tanya is a Bargaining Unit Employee under the CWA Orange Contract, so she's an Eligible Employee, and hired in 2010, she cleared the 3-year vesting bar in 2013. Her benefit is hers to keep even if she leaves. Note she's not legacy management (she wasn't employed on Dec 31, 2005), so her eligibility flows from the bargaining agreement, not the management path, a distinction worth confirming, since post-2005 management hires land in the Management Cash Balance Program instead.

Section 03 · The Pension

One formula: the Cash Balance Account

This is the whole pension. Unlike the legacy AT&T plans (which pick the greatest of several formulas or add a Pre-99 band on top), the Mobility pension is a single hypothetical account, and there is no Modified Rule of 75 and no early-retirement reduction to plan around.

The one thing to internalize

The Mobility benefit is the Cash Balance Account. Whatever the account is worth is what the client has, payable as a lump sum or converted to an annuity. Early or late, the starting value is the account, there is no subsidy for retiring at a "magic" age and service combination.

The account is hypothetical: there's no segregated pot of money, but the recordkeeper tracks a running balance that determines the benefit. It starts at $0 at participation and grows from two kinds of credits:

The advisor's lens

Because the pay credit is a flat percentage of pay (below), the Mobility account grows steadily rather than accelerating late in a career the way the legacy age-stepped cash balance did. There's no "give up your highest-value accrual years" argument here, so the retire-or-stay conversation turns on 401(k) growth, Social Security timing, and healthcare, not a pension cliff.

Client example: Tanya Brooks

For Tanya there's no "greatest of three" to decode and no Pre-99 band, whatever her Cash Balance Account is worth on the day she commences is her pension, payable as a lump sum or converted to an annuity. There's no magic age-and-service combination that suddenly boosts the pension, so the conversation with Tanya is about growing and rolling the account, not timing a subsidy. The one milestone that does matter for her is the healthcare test in Section 08, which lives in a different plan entirely.

Section 04

Cash balance mechanics

① Basic Benefit Credit: a flat 5%

Each month the account is credited with 5% of Pension Compensation (credited on the last day of the month, or the termination/transfer date if earlier).

Pension Compensation here is broad: W-2 wages plus before-tax elective deferrals under IRC §§125, 457, and 132(f), plus before-tax salary deferrals to a nonqualified deferred-comp plan.

Worked example

Monthly Pension Compensation of $5,000 → Basic Benefit Credit = 5% × $5,000 = $250 added that month.

② Interest Credit: steady compounding

Each month, before that month's Basic Benefit Credit is added, the prior month-end balance earns interest at the applicable rate (the monthly equivalent of the 30-Year U.S. Treasury rate, published as of the second month of the prior calendar quarter). Interest credits continue after termination until the month before payments begin.

Worked example

Prior balance $10,000; this month's Basic Benefit Credit $200; interest rate ~4% annual. Interest ≈ $32.70 on the prior balance. New balance = $10,000 + $200 + $32.70 = $10,232.70.

Rate varies, confirm it

The interest crediting rate tracks the 30-Year Treasury and resets each quarter. Don't quote a fixed rate to a client, pull the current crediting rate from the Recordkeeper and the client's actual balance from NetBenefits.

Client example: Tanya Brooks

Tanya's monthly Pension Compensation is $5,000, so her Basic Benefit Credit is 5% × $5,000 = $250 this month, a flat 5%, the same whether she's 30 or 60. Her account entered the month at about $70,000; at an illustrative ~4% annual crediting rate the interest credit is roughly $70,000 × 0.327% ≈ $229. New balance ≈ $70,000 + $229 + $250 = $70,479. Because the pay credit is flat, Tanya's account grows steadily rather than accelerating late in her career, and confirm the current quarter's crediting rate rather than quoting the 4%.

Section 05

How the pension gets paid

The client can take the account as a lump sum or convert it to a lifetime annuity. Once annuity payments begin, the form is permanent, so an annuity election is a decision to get right the first time.

Lump Sum

Always available
  • Equals the full Cash Balance Account on the BCD
  • Rollable to IRA, tax-deferred
  • Investable & inheritable
  • Subject to RMDs later
  • Longevity & market risk shift to client

Single Life Annuity

Covers one lifetime
  • Highest monthly payment; PBGC-guaranteed
  • Stops at death, nothing for survivor
  • No inflation adjustment

Joint & Survivor (50 / 75%)

Covers two lifetimes
  • Continues to spouse/partner; PBGC-guaranteed
  • Pop-up always applies
  • Lower monthly payment; no 100% J&S option
Key differences from the legacy plans

The Mobility Program offers no 100% Joint & Survivor option (only 50% and 75%). The pop-up feature (your benefit restores to the single-life level if your survivor dies first) always applies here, it is not conditioned on any Rule of 75. And a Joint & Survivor election cannot be waived by a QDRO in this program.

How the annuity is derived

An annuity is the account divided by an age-based factor (using the 1994 Group Annuity Reserve mortality table and the 30-Year Treasury rate). The SPD's illustration:

Worked example

A $100,000 account at age 65, with an annuity factor of 141.5292, converts to a Single Life Annuity of $706.57/month ($100,000 ÷ 141.5292).

For the 50% J&S: if the client first became a Participant on/after Jan 1, 2006, the amount is the Actuarial Equivalent (based on both ages); if they were a Participant as of Dec 31, 2005, it is the greater of 90% of the SLA or the Actuarial Equivalent. The 75% J&S is Actuarial Equivalent. Factors change with rates, verify.

Small-benefit cash-out

If the present value is small (≤ $1,000 is paid automatically as a single lump sum; over $1,000 to $7,000 is an automatic IRA rollover unless the client elects otherwise), the normal election rules don't apply. This upper threshold rose from $5,000 to $7,000 effective Jan 1, 2024 (2024 SMM). Flag this so a client isn't surprised.

The advisor's lens: lump sum vs. annuity

Since the lump sum is always on the table and equals the account, the core decision is whether to roll it to an IRA for growth, flexibility, and legacy or annuitize for a guaranteed floor. Weigh health and longevity, the survivor's own income, other guaranteed income, and the annuity's implied payout rate versus what a diversified portfolio could support. A direct rollover avoids the mandatory 20% federal withholding that hits a lump sum paid directly to the client.

No interest-rate timing to worry about

Unlike the legacy defined-benefit pensions (where a lump sum is an annuity converted with IRS segment rates, and rising rates shrink the payout), the Mobility lump sum is simply the Cash Balance Account. It is not rate-converted, so there's no "commence before rates rise" timing game, the only rate that touches it is the monthly interest credit (Section 04). One less variable to model.

Client example: Tanya Brooks

Suppose Tanya's account grows to $100,000 by age 65. As a lump sum that's simply $100,000, rollable to an IRA (a direct rollover avoids the mandatory 20% withholding). Or she converts it to an annuity: at the SPD's age-65 factor of 141.5292, $100,000 ÷ 141.5292 = a $706.57/month Single Life Annuity. Married, she could instead take a 50% or 75% J&S (there's no 100% option here), and the pop-up always applies, unlike the legacy plans it isn't conditioned on any Rule of 75. Note a J&S election in this program can't be waived by a QDRO.

Section 06 · The Savings Plan

The AT&T 401(k)

Mobility employees participate in the AT&T Retirement Savings Plan (ARSP, Plan 009, SPD NIN 78-74968, eff. July 2026) at Fidelity. The employee funds it and directs the investments, and AT&T adds a company match. For most Mobility clients this, plus the rollover of the cash balance account, is where the real planning lives.

Company match for Mobility

Match applies to Basic Contributions up to 6% of Compensation, with eligibility beginning upon hire for the Mobility bargained contracts (Orange, Purple, Black) and, since Jan 1, 2024, for Mobility Blue (IBEW Local 1547) as well, which previously required one Year of Service. The formula varies by contract: commonly 80% of the first 6% (~4.8% of pay), while some groups (Mobility Black Contract, CWA District 3 hired/rehired/transferred ≥1/1/15, and former DIRECTV employees regardless of hire date; Mobility Blue Contract, IBEW Local 1547 hired/rehired/transferred ≥1/1/12) receive 100% of the first 6%. Catch-up contributions are not matched. Match vests 100% after 3 Years of Service. Confirm the client's contract tier in NetBenefits.

One documentation quirk worth knowing rather than resolving: the July 2026 SPD describes the Purple Contract as CWA District 3 in its detailed special rule but as District 6 in its summary table, and it lists District 5 among the Mobility Orange districts in its eligibility section while omitting District 5 from the immediate-match list. Confirm the client's actual bargaining unit rather than relying on either list.

Plan change: Roth and non-Roth invested separately (September 2026)

The AT&T Retirement Savings Plan is being enhanced so Roth and non-Roth balances can be invested differently. Fidelity's Summary of Material Modifications and blackout notice, issued against the July 2026 SPD, sets the effective date at September 21, 2026. Two things change together: investment elections in the core lineup (the Standard Plan Options) can be set separately for Roth and non-Roth money, and every Fidelity BrokerageLink account under the plan is split in two, BrokerageLink for non-Roth balances and BrokerageLink Roth for Roth balances.

In the notice, "non-Roth" means every other contribution type and its earnings: before-tax, regular after-tax, before-tax catch-up, and employer money. Only Roth after-tax and Roth after-tax catch-up dollars count as Roth.

Time-sensitive: BrokerageLink blackout, Sept 14 to Sept 22, 2026

From approximately 4 p.m. Eastern on September 14, 2026 until approximately 9:30 a.m. Eastern on September 22, 2026, a client cannot make purchases in BrokerageLink or exchange into or out of it. Sell orders are still allowed and settle into the core position, Fidelity Government Cash Reserves. Open orders on the existing BrokerageLink account are canceled on or about September 14 after 4 p.m. Eastern and are not re-established. Because exchanges out are blocked, this can also hold up a distribution the client intends to fund with BrokerageLink assets. The Standard Plan Options are unaffected: every other investment and transaction stays available.

Which clients are affected

①

Standard Plan Options only

No action required and no blackout. The news is new flexibility: from Sept 21 the client can invest Roth balances and future Roth contributions differently from non-Roth money. Opening a BrokerageLink account is not required.

②

BrokerageLink, no Roth balance

An empty BrokerageLink Roth account is created automatically. The client can keep exchanging and trading as usual through the change, and the new account opens for transactions at market open on Sept 22, 2026.

③

BrokerageLink with a Roth balance

The Roth share of the account is prorated and transferred in kind to BrokerageLink Roth, and the blackout applies. This is the group that needs a conversation before Sept 14.

To check which group a client is in: NetBenefits → the savings plan account → Summary tab → "Show Details" under the Sources chart, then expand each Roth source (Roth, Roth Catch-Up, Roth Rollover) and look for a BrokerageLink balance. There can be more than one Roth source, so check them all.

What does not carry over to the new account

Must be re-established on BrokerageLink Roth

These features do not transfer automatically, even when they already exist on the current BrokerageLink account:

Third-party authorization. Any third-party access, including advisor access, requires new paperwork on the BrokerageLink Roth account. For any client whose BrokerageLink account we're authorized on today, this is the item most likely to interrupt service, so get the paperwork moving ahead of the transfer.

Automatic investment of payroll contributions. If BrokerageLink contributions are automatically invested into preselected mutual funds, that instruction has to be set up separately on the Roth account.

Dividend reinvestment elections. Mutual fund dividends reinvest in the same fund either way, but dividends from other eligible securities default to the core position, Fidelity Government Cash Reserves, unless the client enrolls in the dividend reinvestment program on the new account.

Cost basis. Cost basis does not carry over on transferred securities, so gain and loss since the original purchase can no longer be tracked in the new account. This does not change how a distribution from the plan is taxed.

How Fidelity splits an existing account

The Roth balance is calculated by combining the cost basis of all Roth contributions and their associated earnings, with share prices set two business days before the transfer. That balance is divided by the balance of eligible divisible securities to produce a Roth percentage, which is then applied to every divisible holding to decide how many shares move across. Rights and warrants, unpriced or worthless securities, restricted securities, and fractional shares outside the dividend reinvestment program are excluded from the calculation. Shares round to three decimals (two for core cash), while bonds, bills, notes, and CDs round down to the nearest 1,000-unit increment and drop out of the calculation entirely below 1,000 units. Any overage or shortage of $100 or less is trued up in core cash; more than $100 triggers a second proration on what's left. An account that is 100% Roth simply transfers in kind.

Expect cosmetic side effects: a round lot can become an odd lot, trailing dividends on a transferred position post to and stay in the existing account, and a resulting position of less than one whole share is liquidated unless the client has opted into fractional share trading.

The hard deadline: unsplittable holdings get sold

If a preliminary review finds a holding that has to be liquidated for the Roth and non-Roth balances to separate, Fidelity will attempt to contact the client. If the client has not acted by September 14, 2026, AT&T has directed Fidelity to sell BrokerageLink assets on their behalf. The selling order is mutual funds first (highest balance, and on a tie the higher share price), then equities on the same rule, then fixed income (lowest market value first, bonds before CDs). In most cases the amount is $3.00 or less, and above that the sale is capped at the amount needed plus a 10% buffer for market movement. Trade expenses and any realized gain or loss are borne by the existing BrokerageLink account, though commissions on these trades are waived while that stays administratively feasible. Concentrated or illiquid positions are where this bites, so review BrokerageLink holdings with the client well before Sept 14.

Investing Roth and non-Roth differently in the Standard Plan Options

Today one investment election generally applies across every eligible contribution source. From Sept 21, 2026 there are two separate paths on NetBenefits.

Future Roth contributions

Change Investments → Investment Elections → Change Your Investment Elections, then choose the elections for the Roth 401(k) source. This governs new money only.

The existing Roth balance

Change Investments → Exchanges, choose the investment and amount, then select the applicable source or source group and complete the transaction. This moves money already in the account.

Both steps are needed to fully separate a client's Roth strategy from their non-Roth strategy: the first only redirects future contributions, the second only repositions the current balance.

The advisor's lens

Two very different conversations come out of one notice. For clients in the core lineup it's an asset-location opportunity: until now a single election applied to every source, so the Roth and before-tax buckets were forced into the same allocation, and after Sept 21 they can be positioned independently. Mobility clients skew younger, so the Roth bucket is often the one with the longest runway. For clients with BrokerageLink it's operational risk on a deadline: advisor authorization has to be re-papered on the new account, automatic investment and dividend elections have to be rebuilt, and anything that can't be split cleanly gets sold if nobody acts by Sept 14. AT&T's notice is explicit that nothing in it is a recommendation about how Roth or non-Roth balances should be invested, that judgment sits with the client and their advisor.

Catch-up contributions: Roth is now mandatory for higher earners

The SECURE 2.0 Roth-catch-up rule is in force in the ARSP now. Under the July 2026 SPD, a participant who is age 50 or older by the end of the year and earned more than $150,000 in FICA wages in 2025 must make catch-up contributions on a Roth basis only. There is no bargained-plan deferral attached, so treat it as live for Mobility's bargained population. A senior technician with heavy overtime can cross $150,000 without thinking of themselves as a high earner, so check the wage figure rather than the base rate.

Catch-up requires a separate election in 1% increments and will not be accepted unless the Basic Contribution election is above 0%. It is excluded from spillover, so it simply stops at the annual limit rather than continuing as after-tax. Clients ages 60–63 are defaulted toward the higher $11,250 limit and must log in to NetBenefits to reduce it.

Two more limits that bite before 415(c) does

The plan counts only the first $360,000 of Compensation in 2026, and the SPD states that on reaching that cap, contributions are automatically suspended for the rest of the year, which can silently stop the per-pay-period match before December. Separately, a client whose 2025 Compensation exceeded $160,000 is a highly compensated employee for 2026 and can have contributions or match reduced, recharacterized, or partially returned through nondiscrimination testing, so an aggressive after-tax target is a target rather than a guarantee.

After-tax contributions → the "mega backdoor Roth"

The AT&T Retirement Savings Plan SPD confirms the pieces this needs: after-tax contributions are permitted, and a Roth In-Plan Rollover lets the client convert eligible before-tax and after-tax money to Roth within the plan. Converting after-tax dollars promptly means little or no taxable growth has built up, so there's little or no tax on the conversion, and the money then grows tax-free. The conversion is irrevocable, confirm current elections in NetBenefits.

Front-loading can forfeit match

The plan matches Basic Contributions up to 6% of Compensation each pay period and the SPD describes no "true-up," so any pay period under 6% leaves match behind for good. Matched Basic Contributions can be pre-tax, Roth, after-tax, or any combination, and the plan's default spillover election softens the classic front-loading trap: once the deferral limit is hit, contributions automatically continue as after-tax and keep earning match. But if the client has opted out of spillover, or suspends contributions after front-loading, the per-paycheck match stops with them, leaving employer money on the table. Contribute at least 6% every pay period and confirm the spillover election in NetBenefits.

At separation: two traps to handle

The Rule of 55, and how a rollover forfeits it

If a client separates from AT&T in the year they turn 55 or older, they can take withdrawals from the AT&T 401(k) penalty-free (no 10% early-withdrawal penalty) while the money stays in the plan, a valuable bridge for an early retiree before age 59½. Rolling the 401(k) to an IRA forfeits this: an IRA has no Rule of 55, so the client is back to the 59½ threshold. Before recommending a full rollover for a 55–59 client, check whether they'll need penalty-free access first, it can be worth leaving some or all in the plan. Note the plan may also limit how those withdrawals can be taken (e.g., not flexible monthly payments), confirm with Fidelity.

Handle an outstanding 401(k) loan before rolling over

If the client has a 401(k) loan when they leave, address it deliberately, three paths: (1) Pay it off before the rollover (allow ~72 hours for the $0 balance to post, or the rollover can inadvertently trigger a default); (2) Let it default, the outstanding balance is issued on a 1099-R as a taxable distribution (plus the 10% penalty if under 59½ with no exemption); or (3) Keep it by leaving enough balance in the plan and continuing payments. Flag this early, an overlooked loan is a common, avoidable tax surprise at rollover.

The advisor's lens

Advising an AT&T Mobility employee well means working across the full benefits stack rather than the 401(k) alone, with the Fidelity-held funds under active management. Coordinate the cash-balance rollover and the 401(k) so the household's Fidelity assets are managed together. Make sure contribution rate, full match capture, and allocation are all working before layering on after-tax → Roth strategies.

Client example: Tanya Brooks

Tanya earns $70,000. To capture her full match she contributes at least 6% each pay period, $4,200/year. On the Orange Contract's common formula of 80% of the first 6%, her employer adds 80% × $4,200 = $3,360/year (confirm her exact contract tier in NetBenefits). If she front-loads and drops below 6% late in the year, and has opted out of spillover, those under-6% paychecks leave match behind with no true-up. Earlier in her career and likely in a moderate bracket, Tanya leans Roth on the deferral, and any headroom below the $72,000 415(c) ceiling can go to after-tax contributions converted via the plan's Roth In-Plan Rollover.

Section 07

IRMAA planning

Once a client reaches Medicare, premiums are no longer flat. The Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA) adds a surcharge to Medicare Part B and Part D premiums when modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) crosses a threshold. Pension/annuity income, 401(k) and IRA withdrawals, and Roth conversions all feed MAGI, so how a client sequences distributions directly drives what they pay for Medicare.

FeatureWhat it means for planning
Two-year lookback2026 premiums are set by 2024 MAGI. A distribution or conversion today shows up in premiums ~2 years later.
Cliff, not a rampCrossing a threshold by even $1 triggers the full surcharge for that tier. The dollar that puts a client over can carry a very high effective marginal cost.
Per personFor a married couple, both Medicare-enrolled spouses pay the surcharge once joint MAGI crosses a tier.

For reference, 2026 IRMAA begins above $109,000 MAGI (single) and $218,000 (married filing jointly), on a five-tier sliding scale (topping out at $500,000 single / $750,000 MFJ). Thresholds change yearly, always pull current figures: medicare.gov: Medicare costs & IRMAA.

The advisor's lens

Because a Mobility client often rolls the cash balance to an IRA, the retirement income picture is largely IRA/401(k) withdrawals plus Social Security, highly controllable for MAGI purposes. That control is an opportunity: project MAGI each year and size withdrawals and Roth conversions to stay just under the next bracket or IRMAA tier, especially in the low-income window between retiring and the start of Social Security and RMDs.

Not advice: a framework

Conversion and distribution strategy depend on the individual's full tax picture, state taxes, legacy goals, and assumptions about future rates. Use these as factors to evaluate with the client and their tax professional. This is not tax advice.

Client example: Tanya Brooks

Because Tanya rolls her cash balance to an IRA, her retirement income is mostly IRA/401(k) withdrawals plus Social Security, highly controllable for MAGI. Say she files single with MAGI around $90,000, under the $109,000 single-filer IRMAA threshold. In a low-income year before RMDs, a $15,000 Roth conversion lands her at $105,000, still under the line; a $25,000 conversion would push her to $115,000 and trip the full first-tier surcharge two years later. That controllability is the opportunity: project MAGI each year and size withdrawals and conversions to sit just under the next bracket or IRMAA tier.

Section 08 · Advisor Toolkit

Retiree healthcare

Here is the subtle, high-value point for Mobility clients: even though the Mobility pension has no Modified Rule of 75, retiree medical eligibility is decided by the Medical Program's own age-and-service test, which uses the same Rule-of-75 combinations. So a Mobility employee can still earn retiree-medical access by hitting that milestone.

How eligibility actually works

The AT&T Medical Program SPD grants retiree ("Post-Employment") medical eligibility to a former Management, Bargained, or Nonmanagement-Nonunion employee who meets an age-and-Term-of-Employment combination at termination:

Minimum ageTerm of Employment required
Any age30 years
5025 years
5520 years
65 or older10 years

Age and Term of Employment use completed whole years, tested at termination. This test lives in the Medical Program and is independent of the fact that the Mobility pension has no early-retirement subsidy.

Why it matters most for pre-65 retirees

A client who retires before age 65 is not yet Medicare-eligible. Individual coverage in that gap can run well over $1,000–$2,000/month per person. The AT&T Medical Program is a pre-Medicare bridge: an eligible retiree can enroll until becoming Medicare-eligible, then transitions off (to a private exchange, with possible Medicare Part B/Part D premium reimbursement). A client leaving a few months short of the age/service milestone can lose this access entirely.

Access is not the same as "subsidized"

Meeting the test opens eligibility to enroll, not a fixed price, and the subsidy is now narrow. The retiree pays a monthly contribution toward the Cost of Coverage:

Hired on/after 1/1/2001 → 100% of cost (no subsidy). Only pre-2001 hires may qualify, under legacy-company rules.

65+ Medicare subsidy: eliminated for anyone (bargained or management) with a last day on/after 1/1/2021. A management-side pre-Medicare cut also applies for last day on/after 1/1/2022. Mobility covers both bargained and legacy-management populations, so check which rules apply to the specific client.

Enrollment runs through Alight. Contributions and plan tiers (Bronze/Silver/Gold) are in the separate Medical Program SPD and change over time, confirm the client's actual contribution in their Total Reward statement before quoting a figure.

Client example: Tanya Brooks

Here's the milestone that does matter for Tanya. At 51 with 16 years, she doesn't yet meet any age/service combination. But if she stays to 55 with 20 years (around 2030), she satisfies the Medical Program's test and earns eligibility to enroll in retiree medical, the pre-Medicare bridge from 55 to 65. Leave at 51/16 and she loses that access entirely; the pension is identical either way, but the healthcare door closes. One caveat: hired in 2010 (after 1/1/2001), she'd pay 100% of the Cost of Coverage, the value is guaranteed access to group coverage, not a subsidy.

Confirm this is the right program

Section 10

Protections & rules to know

PBGC insurance

The pension is federally insured by the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation, up to statutory limits. A lump sum forfeits this guarantee.

QDRO (divorce)

A Qualified Domestic Relations Order can assign part of the benefit to a former spouse. Note the Joint & Survivor annuity cannot be waived by QDRO in this program.

Survivor benefits before commencement

A spouse may elect an SLA equal to the greater of 50% of the day-before-death 50% J&S benefit or the actuarial-equivalent SLA of the account, or a single lump sum of the account. With no spouse/partner/beneficiary, the account is paid to the estate.

No Retiree Death Benefit

Unlike the legacy Nonbargained Program, the Mobility Program has no separate Retiree Death Benefit. The survivor value is the account itself.

Rehire & portability

Rehire and controlled-group transfer rules offset prior distributions and prevent double counting. Note the Mandatory Portability Agreement provisions do not apply while employed by AT&T Mobility Services LLC.

Direct rollover

A lump-sum eligible rollover distribution can roll to an IRA or eligible plan; otherwise 20% mandatory federal withholding applies.

Client example: Tanya Brooks

If Tanya died before commencing, her husband could elect a Single Life Annuity, the greater of 50% of the day-before-death 50% J&S benefit or the actuarial-equivalent SLA of the account, or take the account as a single lump sum; with no spouse, partner, or beneficiary, the account goes to her estate. Unlike the legacy Nonbargained Program, there's no separate Retiree Death Benefit here, the survivor value is the account itself, so keeping her Fidelity beneficiary designation current is the whole ballgame. And remember her J&S annuity can't be waived by a QDRO in this program.

Section 11

Glossary

Cash Balance Account
The hypothetical account that is the Mobility pension; grows via Basic Benefit Credits and Interest Credits.
Basic Benefit Credit
Monthly credit equal to 5% of Pension Compensation.
Pension Compensation (Mobility)
W-2 wages plus before-tax elective deferrals under IRC §§125/457/132(f) and before-tax nonqualified deferred-comp deferrals. Broader than the legacy CAM definition.
Interest Credit
Monthly credit at the applicable rate (compounding to the 30-Year U.S. Treasury rate, from the second month of the prior quarter) on the prior month-end balance; continues after termination until payments begin.
Normal Retirement Age
A flat age 65 in the Mobility Program.
Actuarial Equivalent
The conversion basis for annuities and lump sums, using the 1994 GAR mortality table and the 30-Year Treasury rate (annuities) or the statutory rate (lump sums).
Single Life Annuity (SLA)
Account ÷ an age-based annuity factor; highest monthly payment, nothing to a survivor.
Joint & Survivor (J&S)
An annuity continuing 50% or 75% to a survivor (no 100% option); the pop-up always applies and it cannot be waived by QDRO.
Pop-up
Restoration of the retiree's benefit to the SLA level if the survivor dies first; always applies in the Mobility Program.
402(g) limit
The annual cap on an employee's own elective deferrals (pre-tax + Roth). $24,500 in 2026.
415(c) limit
The annual cap on all additions to the plan combined. $72,000 in 2026.
Roth In-Plan Rollover
The plan feature that converts eligible before-tax and after-tax money to Roth within the plan; irrevocable.
Mega-backdoor Roth
Making after-tax contributions and converting them to Roth, turning 415(c) headroom into tax-free Roth money.
IRMAA
Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount, an income-based surcharge on Medicare Part B and D premiums, set by MAGI from two years prior.
PBGC
Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation, federal insurer of the pension.
SPD
Summary Plan Description, the controlling plain-language plan document.
SMM
Summary of Material Modifications, the notice that amends an SPD between full reissues. The Roth investment enhancements arrive as an SMM to the July 2026 SPD.
Standard Plan Options
The savings plan's core investment lineup, the funds offered directly by the employer, as distinct from the brokerage window.
BrokerageLink / BrokerageLink Roth
Fidelity's self-directed brokerage window inside the plan. From Sept 21, 2026 it exists as two accounts, BrokerageLink for non-Roth balances and BrokerageLink Roth for Roth balances.
Blackout period
More than three consecutive business days in which the right to direct or diversify plan balances, or to take loans or distributions, is suspended or restricted. The BrokerageLink blackout runs Sept 14 to Sept 22, 2026.
