Your information

Pick your group and whether you're still working or have already left AT&T. Then enter either exact dates or just your age and years of service, whatever you have handy. Either way you'll get the same list of dates (ages and years show as "on or about").

Please read this first

This tool shows general, dated milestones based on federal rules and your AT&T plan. It assumes you worked without a break from your hire date until you leave, and it can't see things like a break in service, a rehire, disability, or the exact terms of your contract. Dollar amounts are 2026 figures. Always check your real numbers in your Fidelity NetBenefits account and at ssa.gov and medicare.gov, and talk with your advisor, before making a decision.

One date that isn't about your age: September 2026

AT&T is changing the savings plan so your Roth money and your non-Roth money (before-tax, after-tax, and the company match) can be invested differently. The change takes effect September 21, 2026. If all of your 401(k) is in the regular fund lineup, you don't have to do anything: you simply get the option to invest your Roth money differently starting that day.

If you use BrokerageLink, the brokerage window inside the plan, there's more to it. Your BrokerageLink account becomes two accounts, one for Roth and one for everything else, and you won't be able to buy anything in BrokerageLink, or move money into or out of it, from about 4 p.m. Eastern on September 14 until about 9:30 a.m. Eastern on September 22, 2026. You can still sell during that stretch, and the rest of the plan works normally. Any open orders you have are canceled on September 14 and won't come back, and if you have anything in there that can't be split between Roth and non-Roth, Fidelity may sell it for you if you haven't acted by September 14. Anyone you've authorized on the account, including your advisor, has to be re-authorized on the new Roth account with new paperwork.

Worth a call to Fidelity at 800-890-4015 (Mon–Fri, 8:30 a.m.–8 p.m. Eastern) or to your advisor before September 14 if you hold anything in BrokerageLink.

Section 03 · When you leave

When you leave AT&T

What your last day at AT&T sets in motion: the things to sort out once you know (or are choosing) when to leave.

Why your last day matters most

Your last day at AT&T is the one big date you control. Leaving even a few months before a key milestone, the Rule of 75, the year you turn 55, or your 3-year vesting date, can cost you a subsidized pension, penalty-free access to your 401(k), or the chance to buy AT&T retiree health coverage. Sometimes that's tens of thousands of dollars. Check your dates against the timeline above before you give notice.

Milestones by age

Rule of 75Age + service reaches one of: 30 yrs any age · 50 with 25 · 55 with 20 · 65 with 10Union & Legacy Non-Bargained: unlocks a subsidized early pension. All groups incl. Mobility: it's also how AT&T decides who can buy retiree health coverage.

55Rule of 55 · pension ageLeave in or after the year you turn 55 and you can take money from your AT&T 401(k) with no 10% penalty (moving it to an IRA gives this up). It's also when the early-pension cut ends (56 for Southeast contracts).
59½Penalty ends everywhereThe 10% early-withdrawal penalty ends for all 401(k)s and IRAs, whether or not you're still working.
60–63Bigger 401(k) catch-up$11,250 (2026) replaces the usual extra amount for the years you turn 60 through 63; it drops back at 64.
62Earliest Social SecurityYou can claim, but the check is smaller for life (about 30% less than at full age 67). If you're still working, part can be held back. ssa.gov
63Medicare surcharge starts countingYour Medicare premiums at 65 are based on your income from two years earlier, so the year you turn 63 is the first year a big income spike (like a large Roth conversion) can raise them. In 2026 it starts above $109,000 single / $218,000 married. medicare.gov
64¾Medicare sign-up windowA 7-month window opens 3 months before the month you turn 65. Miss it (without job coverage) and you can pay a lifetime Part B penalty.
65Medicare · coverage handoffYour pre-65 coverage (AT&T retiree plan or COBRA) hands off to Medicare plus a supplement. COBRA does not count as job coverage for delaying Part B. Mobility: this is also the plan's normal retirement age.
Full age 66–67Social Security full retirement ageBy birth year: 1954 or earlier 66; 1955–59 66 + 2 months per year; 1960+ 67. Full benefit, and no limit on working. ssa.gov
70Social Security maxes outYour benefit grows about 8% a year until 70, then stops growing. No reason to wait longer.
70½Give to charity from your IRAYou can donate straight from your IRA (a QCD). It isn't counted as income (which helps with the Medicare surcharge) and counts toward your required withdrawals once those start. irs.gov
73 / 75Required withdrawals beginBorn 1951–1959: age 73. Born 1960 or later: age 75. You must start taking a set amount each year from your pre-tax accounts. The first one can wait until April 1 of the next year, but then you take two in one year. Roth IRAs and (since 2024) Roth 401(k)s are exempt. irs.gov

If you were born in 1959, the law was unclear whether your required withdrawals start at 73 or 75; the IRS's proposed rule says 73, so confirm your exact age before relying on it.
